M.Tech Cyber Security Academic Project

Graph-Based Smart Car Parking Allocation and Route Optimization with Online Slot Booking for Gandhipuram, Coimbatore

Course: Mathematics for Cyber Security | Comprehensive System Documentation & Methodology

1. Problem Statement & Motivation

Gandhipuram is one of the most densely populated commercial and transportation epicenters in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, housing the central intercity bus terminal, Cross Cut Road retail corridor, 100 Feet commercial avenue, and Dr. Nanjappa Road arterial highway. Cruising for on-street parking in this zone induces severe traffic bottlenecks, exacerbates carbon emissions, and wastes driver commute time.

Conventional parking solutions either rely on uncoordinated roadside cruising or naive first-come allocations that neglect network topology and system-level social travel distance. This project addresses the challenge by formulating an integrated, capacity-constrained cyber-physical smart parking architecture backed by graph-theoretic road analysis, minimum-cost maximum-flow optimization, and heuristic shortest-path routing.

2. Graph-Theoretic Road Network Representation

The physical road topology of Gandhipuram is modeled as a weighted directed multigraph $G = (V, E)$, acquired from OpenStreetMap within a 1,400-meter radius centered at coordinates (11.0182714°N, 76.9677744°E):

  • Vertices $V$: $1,390$ road intersections and spatial decision points with geographic attributes $(\text{lat}_u, \text{lon}_u)$.
  • Directed Edges $E$: $3,406$ directed road segments preserving one-way vehicular direction restrictions.
  • Edge Weights $w(u, v)$: Exact arc length in meters ($L_{uv} \in \mathbb{R}^+$).
  • Topological Robustness: Analyzed via 296 articulation points (cut vertices) and 325 bridge edges representing systemic transit bottlenecks.

3. Allocation Algorithms Mathematical Formulation

A. Minimum-Cost Maximum-Flow (MCMF) Formulation

Let $N$ denote the set of vehicle requests and $M$ denote the set of parking facilities. We construct a flow network $F = (V_F, E_F)$ with super-source $S$ and super-sink $T$:

Minimize:   ∑i ∈ N ∑j ∈ M cij · xij

Subject to:
  ∑j ∈ M xij ≤ 1,   ∀ i ∈ N            (Single facility assignment per vehicle)
  ∑i ∈ N xij ≤ Aj,   ∀ j ∈ M            (Facility available capacity constraint)
  xij ∈ {0, 1},                        (Binary decision variable)

where $c_{ij}$ represents the shortest-path travel distance from vehicle $i$'s current position to facility $j$'s access node, and $A_j$ denotes the remaining available capacity of facility $j$. The network simplex algorithm guarantees total travel cost minimization while maximizing assignment cardinality.

B. Nearest Available Parking

For each sequential arrival $i$, the algorithm computes shortest-path distances $d(u_i, p_j)$ to all facilities where remaining capacity $A_j > 0$, greedily assigning $i \to \arg\min_{j : A_j > 0} d(u_i, p_j)$ and decrementing $A_j \leftarrow A_j - 1$.

C. First-Come, First-Served (FCFS)

Vehicles are processed strictly in arrival order and allocated to the first facility with $A_j > 0$ according to a fixed operational indexing (P1 → P2 → P3 → P4 → P5). This baseline models uncoordinated static zoning.

4. Shortest-Path Route Optimization

Vehicle routing executes over directed graph $G$. We evaluate Dijkstra's priority-queue search against $A^*$ search guided by the spherical Haversine distance heuristic $h(u, v)$:

Heuristic Admissibility & Consistency: Because the straight-line great-circle distance $d_{\text{haversine}}(u, v)$ is strictly lower-bound bounded by any physical road traversal distance ($\forall u, v: h(u, v) \le d_{\text{road}}(u, v)$ and $h(u, v) \le c(u, w) + h(w, v)$), $A^*$ is mathematically guaranteed to find the true shortest path while pruning substantial search space.

6. Simulation Assumptions & Academic Scope

  • Simulated Parking Facilities: The 5 facilities (P1–P5) and 150 individual slots are simulated models established for academic evaluation. Their coordinates are snapped to real Gandhipuram road nodes.
  • Traffic Congestion: Edge costs currently reflect physical road metric distance; dynamic traffic flow and signal wait times represent future telemetry integrations.
  • Non-Dominance: While A* search explores ~43% fewer nodes than Dijkstra on long queries, Dijkstra is simpler for dense point-to-all calculations.
